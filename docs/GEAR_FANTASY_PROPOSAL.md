# Gear as the core fantasy — proposal for review

Status: **proposal, nothing decided.** Written 2026-11-17 for Ákos to review.
It changes nothing in the plan until he says so.

## The idea, in your words

- "Mainly revolve around the fantasy of gear and hoarding loot and kinda like surviving
  by maintaining gear and bringing a lot of spares."
- "Battle Brothers did it amazing, this feeling that I'm describing", but "I want
  something MORE engaging and detailed."
- "Crafting definitely interests me, but not sure I wanna pivot altogether."

## Short answer

This is the right kind of game for it. Battle Brothers shows the feeling works in a
turn-based, party-based tactics game, and Elaris has the same bones. Nothing here
needs a different genre.

The important finding for your "not sure I want to pivot": **most of the detail does
not need a pivot.** Section 3 lists six areas of depth; all six fit the game as
planned. A pivot is only needed for the three structural changes in section 2, and
those can be decided later, after the gear layer exists and you can feel it.

---

## 1. What Elaris already has for this

- A party with one shared pack and a weight limit.
- 20 materials and 283 items generated from material × item type, each instance
  carrying its own quality and magic bonus.
- A crafting system: 115 recipes across forge, alchemy table, enchanting table and
  cooking fire.
- Loot drops, a starting kit, a character sheet with equipment slots.
- The shape of an expedition: go into a dungeon, come back out.
- A town planned as a screen, with a smith.
- Durability already agreed as a core mechanic (your homebrew system, with crit
  effects and passive wear by combat length).

## 2. What would have to bend for gear to be THE fantasy

These three are what "pivot" actually means. Each can be taken or left on its own.

**1. Where power comes from.**
In 5e your level is your power and gear is a garnish: a level 5 fighter with a plain
sword beats a level 1 fighter with a magic one. Battle Brothers works because losing
your gear really hurts. For gear to carry the game, levels would stay low and flat
(the 1–5 range already helps), gear would become the main way a character grows, and
resting could not fix everything for free.

**2. Where the detail goes.**
Battle Brothers has almost no written story; its effort went into the loop. Going
deeper than it on gear while also building nine zones, 75 quests, factions and
romances is two games' worth of work. The gear loop would come first, with story as a
thinner layer of reasons to go out.

**3. What a dungeon is.**
It becomes an expedition: you pack for it, your gear wears down inside it, you decide
when to turn back, and you have to carry the loot home. That raises the question of
what you lose when a run goes wrong.

## 3. Where "more engaging and detailed" could go

Places where Battle Brothers stays shallow and Elaris could go deeper.

| Area | What it adds | What exists today | Needs a pivot? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maintenance as decisions** | Field patches versus proper smith repairs; stripping a spare for parts; a camp between fights where time and supplies are limited. Battle Brothers repairs automatically with "tools" | Nothing yet; durability direction agreed | No. Part of durability |
| **Materials and crafting** | Salvaging loot into materials, reforging, upgrading, enchanting. Battle Brothers has almost none | 115 recipes, 4 stations, materials as items. See section 5 | No |
| **Reasons to carry spares** | 5e damage types and resistances make a second weapon a tool, not just a backup: a mace for skeletons, silver for undead | Damage types exist in data but are ignored when damage is applied | No. Phase 2 builds this anyway |
| **Gear drama mid-fight** | A shield splitting, a blade breaking, swapping to a spare, grabbing a dropped weapon, sundering | Your crit rules point here | No. Phase 2 |
| **Loot that remembers the fight** | Enemies drop what they actually wore, in the condition you left it, so how you kill decides what you take home | Loot is rolled from tables; enemies carry nothing | No, but enemies must be given real gear in the Phase 2 bestiary |
| **The hoard** | A town armoury you can see fill up; loadouts planned against what you expect to meet; who carries what | A 25-slot bag | No. Phase 4 town |

## 4. Three levels to choose from

You do not have to choose between "as planned" and "pivot altogether".

### Level A — the planned game, with a deep gear layer

Keep the story RPG. Build all six areas of section 3 into it. 5e progression stays as
it is.

- **You keep:** everything in the current plan.
- **Plan change:** none in order. Crafting depth gets named explicitly in Phases 4–5.
- **Risk:** gear matters, but level matters more. If power does not hinge on gear,
  maintenance can feel like a tax instead of survival.

### Level B — gear-forward

Level A, plus bends 1 and 3 from section 2: flat low levels with gear as the main
growth, rests that do not repair, and dungeons as expeditions with a haul home. The
story stays, but its volume comes after the loop works.

- **You keep:** classes, 5e dice and rules, companions, quests, the world.
- **Plan change:** after Phase 2, loot, maintenance, crafting and the town armoury move
  ahead of class and spell depth. Balance is tuned around gear, not level.
- **Risk:** it stops being "real 5e" in its progression, though not in its dice.

### Level C — full pivot

Level B, plus bend 2: the loop is the game. Story becomes contracts and events; the
nine zones become regions to mount expeditions into; romances, deep faction lines and
most hand-written quests are dropped or deferred.

- **You keep:** the combat, the world's shape, the gear systems.
- **Plan change:** Phase 7 (Zone 1 content) shrinks hard; the content pipeline serves
  items and encounters more than dialogue.
- **Risk:** the game the design document describes is no longer the game.

### What each level asks of the next phases

| | Phase 2 combat | After Phase 2 |
|---|---|---|
| A | As planned | As planned |
| B | As planned, balanced around gear | Loot, maintenance, crafting, armoury first; class and spell depth after |
| C | As planned, balanced around gear | Same as B, and story content cut back |

Phase 2 is the same in all three. That is why the choice can wait.

## 5. Crafting, since it interests you

**What exists today**

- 115 recipes: bronze-to-higher-tier weapons and armour at the forge, potions at the
  alchemy table, +1 to +3 enchants at the enchanting table, food at the cooking fire.
- 47 recipes are known from the start. The other 68 can never be learned: nothing in
  the game teaches a recipe.
- Stations are not placed anywhere; all four sit in one panel.
- No salvaging. Costs ignore item size (a dagger costs the same metal as a greatsword).
- Nothing can craft rapiers, crossbows, scale mail, or any leather or cloth armour.

**What deeper crafting could be.** Each of these fits levels A, B and C.

- **Salvage:** break looted gear into its materials. This is what ties hoarding to
  crafting: everything you carry home is worth something.
- **Repair as crafting:** fixing a piece takes its own material, so spares and scrap
  have a use. Field kits do a rough job; the forge does a proper one.
- **Reforge and upgrade:** raise an item's craftsmanship quality, or remake it in a
  better material. This connects to your notch rule: better material and higher
  bonus mean more notches.
- **Enchanting:** adding a magic bonus, which under your rule also adds notches.
- **Recipe discovery:** bought, looted, learned by salvaging an example, or taught
  by a smith you have befriended.
- **Material properties:** each material behaves differently (light, brittle,
  tough, resists corrosion), so the choice is more than a number.
- **Crafter skill:** 5e tool proficiencies (smith's tools, alchemist's supplies)
  finally mean something: who in the party can do the work, and how well.
- **Where it happens:** stations at their buildings in town, a portable kit for the
  field.

## 6. Recommendation

Do not decide the pivot yet.

Everything in Level A is needed by Levels B and C as well, and Phase 2 is identical in
all three. Build the gear layer first: durability from your homebrew system, gear
events in combat, enemies that carry real gear. Then play it. Whether gear can carry
the whole game is much easier to judge with it in your hands than on paper.

Two things are worth doing from the start, because they are cheap now and awkward to
retrofit:

- Give enemies real equipment in the Phase 2 bestiary, so loot can come from what
  they wore.
- Keep resting and repairing separate, so a rest never quietly fixes gear.
